Introduction and problem statement

Why Indian Sign Language needs open, honest tools

India has millions of Deaf and hard-of-hearing people and very few certified ISL interpreters. Software cannot replace interpreters, but it can help people practise signs, look them up, and communicate simple messages. ISL NOVA is an end-to-end platform built to do that responsibly.

The problem

1 · Scarce, unconsented data

Few ISL datasets are openly licensed, and fewer record who signed and what they agreed to. Training on scraped videos is neither legal nor fair to the signers.

2 · Inflated accuracy claims

Many demos test on the same people they trained on. A high number then says little about how the system works for a new user.

3 · Systems that always guess

A plain classifier always returns some class, even for a sign it never saw, a half-visible hand or an empty frame.

4 · Unverified vocabulary

Glosses copied from a dataset are labels, not reviewed ISL descriptions. Without expert review, a "sign dictionary" can mislead learners.

Objectives

  1. Recognise a 200-sign ISL vocabulary in real time in the browser, with calibrated confidence and explicit rejection of unknown or poor input.
  2. Make every result traceable: licensed source, verification state, data readiness, dataset version, model lineage and checksums.
  3. Collect data consent-first: no recording without consent, no training without model_training consent, and withdrawal erases media.
  4. Evaluate honestly: held-out tests evaluated once, signer-independent splits when signer IDs exist, open-set and new-signer workflows.
  5. Be usable by everyone: learning, communication and kiosk modes, light and dark themes, phone to desktop, accessible by keyboard.

Libraries and models list

Pretrained vision models (Google MediaPipe)

  • hand_landmarker.task (float16): palm detector + 21-keypoint hand model
  • pose_landmarker_lite.task (float16): 33-keypoint body pose
  • Delegates: GPU (WebGL) with automatic CPU (WASM / XNNPACK) fallback

Models trained in this project

  • LSTM: 1 layer, hidden 128, 221,896 parameters (selected)
  • GRU: same shape, about 179k parameters
  • Logistic regression on pooled statistics (scikit-learn)
  • DTW 1-NN: nearest neighbour under dynamic time warping

Why these choices

Landmarks, not pixels

Pose landmarks are small, privacy-friendly and robust to background and clothing, so a compact model trains on CPU in minutes.

NumPy models

No deep-learning framework was installable in the build sandbox, so LSTM/GRU were implemented directly and gradient-checked.

Starlette + esbuild

The tested foundations of FastAPI and Vite; routes and bundling port 1:1 if the project later adopts those tools.

All dependency versions are pinned (backend/requirements.txt, frontend/package.json). A CI audit job runs pip-audit (no known vulnerabilities) and npm audit; the one advisory it found (zod) was fixed by removing the dependency.

Dataset: INCLUDE and the 200-sign vocabulary

Real, licensed recordings, processed reproducibly

INCLUDE at a glance

INCLUDE is a large isolated-sign ISL dataset by A. Sridhar, R. G. Ganesan, P. Kumar and M. Khapra (AI4Bharat, IIT Madras), published at ACM Multimedia 2020 and released on Zenodo (record 4010759) under CC BY 4.0. It contains 263 word signs recorded in 1920 × 1080 at 25 fps.

ISL NOVA uses 200 of those glosses as its vocabulary (ISL001–ISL200). The remaining 63 are held in reserve, and 62 of them now serve as the unknown-sign test set.

Computer vision and feature engineering

From camera pixels to 32 × 204 numbers

Landmark extraction with MediaPipe

2 × 21hand keypoints (x, y, z) per frame
33pose keypoints with visibility
~10–30 fpsin the browser (CPU / GPU)

Frames are processed unmirrored so that left and right hands match between browser and server; only the preview is mirrored for the user. If the GPU delegate runs below 5 fps for three seconds (software WebGL, weak GPUs), the app switches to the CPU delegate automatically and shows a badge.

The feature contract isl-nova-fc-1.0.0

Feature groupWhat it capturesDims
Hand shape21 landmarks per hand, relative to the wrist and scaled by hand size2 × 63

Quality gate

Too few frames or hands visible in too few frames → insufficient_input with a human-readable reason, instead of a guess.

Parity

The TypeScript port is tested against Python fixtures (two hands, one hand, gaps, no pose) to within 2×10-5.

Group sizes are approximate; the authoritative list of all 204 named features is generated from code into docs/FEATURE_CONTRACT.md.

Chapter 4 · Features10
05

Models and algorithms

Four model families, one honest decision rule

LSTM: Long Short-Term Memory selected

A recurrent network that reads the 32 frames in order and keeps a memory of the movement. Final and mean-pooled hidden states feed a 200-way softmax.

  • 1 layer, hidden size 128, 221,896 parameters, 1.78 MB
  • Adam, lr 0.002, weight decay 1e-4, batch 32
  • Augmentation, inverse-√frequency class weights
  • Early stopping on validation (stopped at epoch 49)

GRU: Gated Recurrent Unit

A lighter recurrent network with update and reset gates instead of the LSTM's three gates and cell state.

  • Same shape and recipe as the LSTM
  • About 179k parameters, 1.44 MB
  • Fastest recurrent model (0.17 ms / sample)

Logistic regression on pooled statistics

Each feature's mean, standard deviation, minimum and maximum over time form one vector, classified linearly (scikit-learn).

  • No temporal order, but very strong baseline
  • 0.02 ms / sample, 0.99 MB

DTW 1-NN: Dynamic Time Warping

Finds the training sequence that best aligns with the query when time is stretched or compressed, and copies its label.

  • Batched NumPy DTW with a Sakoe–Chiba window
  • No training, but 130 ms / sample, 64 MB

From scores to an honest answer

Logits200 scores
→
Temperature scalingT = 1.025 fitted on validation
→
Probabilitiescalibrated, ECE 0.027
→
p ≥ τ (0.673) and margin ≥ 0.10recognized
|
p ≥ 0.6 τuncertain
|

τ is chosen on validation so that 95 % of correct validation predictions are kept. The test split never influences any choice. Two further states, insufficient_input and model_unavailable, are returned when the input or the server cannot support a prediction.

Bugs the tests found, and we fixed

  • Windows video upload failed: OpenCV cannot reopen an open temp file on Windows
  • GPU delegate at ~1 fps on software WebGL → automatic CPU switch
  • Silent empty capture → now an explicit insufficient input
  • Fake-camera path must be absolute for Chromium
  • API docs blocked by our own CSP → same-origin init script
  • PostgreSQL FK order on signer erasure (found by CI)
  • Decimal values from PostgreSQL SUM() broke JSON
  • DTW memory blow-up → batched einsum formulation
  • MediaPipe timestamps must be monotonic across videos
  • Mobile overflow of 7–38 px on narrow screens

Security controls

Identity

PBKDF2-SHA256 password hashing, JWT with expiry, server-side RBAC for 5 roles, rate-limited login.

Web

Strict CSP, X-Frame-Options DENY, HSTS in production, no wildcard CORS, upload magic-byte and decode checks.

Data

No secrets in Git, path-safe storage, audited media access, checksummed models and migrations.

Limitations, future work and conclusion

What is proven, what is not, and what comes next

Limitations, stated plainly

  • Results are on INCLUDE's official split, not signer-independent
  • 29 % of unseen signs are still recognized as a known sign
  • No test yet with a real person at a webcam or with new signers
  • 0 / 200 signs verified by qualified ISL reviewers
  • Isolated signs only; no grammar, translation or facial expressions
  • Free demo sleeps when idle and loses its data on restart

Future work

  • Consented recordings from ≥ 10 new signers for a true signer-independent test
  • Stronger unknown-sign detection: energy scores, background class, embeddings
  • TCN and Transformer models; feature ablations; multiple seeds
  • Real-user studies with Deaf signers, with consent and a protocol
  • Verified Hindi and Kannada meanings and regional variants
  • Persistent hosting with managed PostgreSQL

Conclusion

ISL NOVA turns a genuine, licensed ISL dataset into a working, tested and deployed recognition platform. An LSTM trained on 3,370 real INCLUDE recordings recognises all 200 signs with 91.7 % top-1 on held-out videos, and the same model answers correctly through the live public API.

Just as important, the project knows its limits. Each answer carries a calibrated state; unknown-sign rejection, new-signer accuracy and real-webcam use are measured or marked as open; data collection is built around consent; and every number in this report can be traced to the run that produced it.

ISL NOVA 2.0 and 3.0

Three languages, better models, a detector that says "I don't know"

After this report's first edition, the project went through two more rounds (branch feat/isl-nova-v2, merged to main). The vocabulary stays fixed at 200 signs; all work went into making those 200 signs more accurate, more usable and safer. Every number below comes from a GitHub Actions run or a local test that actually ran.

What changed in the models

  • Frame-rate augmentation (v2): training also sees each recording at 8–30 fps, cropped and with missed hand detections. Three-seed mean test top-1 rose from 90.6 % to 93.0 %.
  • New architectures (v3): TCN, Transformer and BiLSTM with attention, written in NumPy with hand-derived gradients that are checked numerically in the test suite. Three seeds each, plus ensembles.
  • Selected model: BiLSTM-attention, 98.1 % validation macro-F1, 95.1 % test top-1, 99.4 % top-5, 2.6 MB.
Chapter 11 · ISL NOVA 2.0 and 3.024

Saying "I don't know"

A small logistic detector combines the model's top probability, the gap to the second sign and the entropy of the prediction. It is fitted on validation signs and one set of unknown glosses, and tested once on a different set. It accepts 8.8 % of unknown signs while holding back 13.2 % of known ones, meeting both targets (under 10 % and at most 15 %). Training an explicit "other" class was also tried and did not do better (16–25 % accepted).

Recognition everywhere

  • On-device: the model and detector also run in the browser (TypeScript), matching the server to 1e-8; recognition keeps working offline.
  • Conversation: signs are split at pauses and shown as a recognized word sequence, never called a translation.
  • Practice Coach: a 0–100 similarity score against real reference recordings (own sign median 93 vs other signs 13).

Still not shown

  • Accuracy on new signers; INCLUDE has no signer IDs, so no result here is signer-independent.
  • A test with a real person at a webcam, and a user study with Deaf users.
  • Linguistic verification: 0 of 200 signs and 0 of 400 Hindi/Kannada meanings verified by experts.
  • Face (non-manual) features were added and tested (contract 2.0.0, +0.7 to +1.3 points for attention models, −0.9 for TCN) but are not the default yet.
Conclusion. ISL NOVA now recognises its 200 signs at 95 % on held-out recordings, says "I don't know" for most signs outside its vocabulary, and works in three languages and offline. What it needs next is people: signers, linguists and users.
